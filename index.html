Capabilities

What we put on a scope

Grouped the way a facilities package reads — VFD and controls work featured, plant cooling and building HVAC supporting it.

Highlighted

VFD retrofits & controls

Variable-frequency drives, motors, and sequences — the work that cuts energy and keeps equipment from hammering itself.

  • VFD retrofits Adding or replacing VFDs on existing HVAC and process motors — fans, pumps, chillers — with proper wiring, parameters, and start-up.
  • Drives & motors Variable-speed drive install, motor change-outs, and troubleshooting on plant and commercial equipment.
  • Automation & controls Controls support on systems we install and service — sequences, sensors, and handoffs that match the mechanical.

Plant cooling & chillers

Heat rejection and chilled-water capacity that keeps process and occupied loads stable.

  • Chiller install New machines for plants, medical campuses, and process cooling loops.
  • Chiller retrofit Change-out, piping tie-in, and controls on live plants with limited downtime.
  • Cooling towers Tower service, repair, and mechanical on the heat-rejection side.

Process utilities & piping

Loops and connections that feed equipment — not just comfort cooling.

  • Process piping Chilled water, process loops, and utility piping for manufacturing and medical sites.
  • Industrial / cleanroom mechanical HV disconnects, utilities, and work inside or adjacent to controlled space.

Building HVAC & air

Occupied-space systems and the air side that supports them.

  • Commercial HVAC / RTUs Rooftops, splits, and building HVAC for manufacturing, medical, and office facilities.
  • Ductwork Install, repair, and modifications for plant, medical, and occupied space.
  • IAQ & ventilation Filtration, ventilation, and pressure-adjacent IAQ for occupied and process spaces.
  • Offices & homes Secondary — office and residential AC/heat across the same service area.

How we work

Downtime discipline, clear scopes

  1. Plan the outage window

    We talk isolation points, temporary cooling if needed, and what “done” looks like before tools hit the floor. Live plants get a sequence — not a surprise.

  2. Write a scope you can hand to facilities

    Equipment, piping, controls, and who’s responsible for what. No vague “HVAC service” line items when the job is a chiller tie-in.

  3. Direct access through delivery

    You deal with the person who scopes and oversees the work. Jobs are scheduled around outage windows, with clear scopes and documentation when the job closes.

About Eco Mechanical

Eco Mechanical LLC is Gildardo Vega’s shop in Maricopa. Roughly fifteen years in the trade — HVAC class of 2010, with hands-on experience scoping and executing plant and commercial mechanical work. Eco Mechanical LLC was founded in 2024: a new company name, not a new tradesman.

Specialty is plant, industrial, medical, cleanroom, and mid-size to large commercial mechanical — chillers, process piping, VFD retrofits, controls, and building HVAC.
